Atlas reads like a printed travel atlas you keep adding to — warm paper and ink, map graticules, stamped monospace labels, and pages where one thing is clearly the subject. This is the locked system behind the v3.1 redesign: foundations, the component kit, and the screen catalogue.

01

Principles

The rules that override individual decisions.
  • A
    Paper & ink, dialled upWarm paper, deep ink, real contrast. Subtle grain and a map grid as texture — never flat.
  • B
    One subject per screenA clear hero — a map, a number, a route — then supporting detail in quieter tiers.
  • C
    Mono is the instrument layerCodes, coordinates, dates and labels in monospace — the cartographer's hand.
  • D
    Data as decorationRoute arcs, duration, distance, aircraft, layovers and timelines do the visual work.
  • E
    A kit with rangeLedgers, rows, panels, feature cards — differentiated on purpose, not one box repeated.
  • F
    Colour always means the sameThe six-state palette drives chips, map fills, timeline nodes and card tints everywhere.

Viatges · list

Trip cards on a real map + route, status as gradient and glass chip.

Viatge · detail

Header with kebab menu, country pills, route map, and route stops with photo slots + excursions.

Vols · list

Distinct itinerary cards above route-hero solo flight cards.
